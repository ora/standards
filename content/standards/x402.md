## How it works

The internet never had a built-in way to charge tiny amounts, so content is either free with ads or behind subscriptions. With x402, a server answers "402: pay $0.01 to this address," the client attaches a signed stablecoin payment (usually USDC), and a "facilitator" settles it on a blockchain in seconds, for fees of a fraction of a cent that card networks can't match.

Picture an agent paying a weather API a cent per lookup, or a crawler paying a publisher per page instead of being blocked.

## Who's behind it

Coinbase created it, and [version 2 shipped on December 11, 2025](https://x402.org/x402-v2-launch/). The Linux Foundation announced an x402 Foundation on April 2, 2026 and [made it operational on July 14](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-operational-launch-of-x402-foundation-to-standardize-internet-native-payments-for-ai-agents-and-applications) with 40 members. It now [lists 57](https://www.x402.org/members); the 19 premier members include AWS, Google, Visa, Mastercard, American Express, PayPal, Stripe, Shopify, Cloudflare, Coinbase and Circle, the broadest coalition of any agent-payment standard.
